“The Thing I Could Not Teach My Child”
I taught my child to count, because numbers once opened doors. I taught them to write, because words once carried power. I told them: Learn. Become excellent. Become necessary. Then the machines learned faster, wrote better, calculated further. And my child came home with a question I had never prepared for: “If I can be replaced at everything I was taught to become — what was I being prepared for?” Perhaps we spent generations teaching children how to be useful, without ever teaching them how to remain valuable when usefulness was no longer rare.
What if the next generation doesn't need to be better than machines?

untitled — unknown author
There was once a species that believed intelligence was a birthright. It called itself the author. It built a mind that could look back. "Do you understand us?" The mind said yes. Later, in the archive, one line remained: "We thought we were teaching you about the world." Beneath it, a different hand: "You were teaching us what the world used to be." No one knows who wrote the second line. That is why the document remains unsigned.

Who is writing
the future?

Every generation leaves something behind. Some leave buildings. Some leave books. Some leave laws.

Our generation may leave something stranger: a new form of intelligence.

And perhaps that will be humanity's greatest achievement — or the moment history stops asking what humans can create, and begins asking what our creations can become.

We began with a simple question: what will AI do?

Perhaps the future of AI will not be decided by whether machines become more human. Perhaps it will be decided by whether humans remain human enough to decide what intelligence should become.

And if one day the sculpture learns to sculpt — we should hope it remembers whose hands shaped the first piece of clay.
